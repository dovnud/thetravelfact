**Short answer:** if you want one trip that covers both countries and the question is weather, go in **January**. It is the only month in which all three of the Australian and New Zealand weather stations on our sheets — [Sydney](/australia/sydney/#weather), [Canberra](/australia/#weather) and [Wellington](/new-zealand/#weather) — pass the plain rule our month lists use. **December** and **February** are the next best, with two of three each. From May to August none of the three passes.

The rest of this story shows why, month by month, and then covers the practical things that change, and the things that don't, when you cross the Tasman Sea.

## How we checked

We did not ask which month *feels* best. We applied the same rule our [month lists](/best-places-to-visit-in-january/) use: a place passes a month when its **average daily high is between 20 °C and 30 °C** and its **average precipitation is under 100 mm**. The numbers come from three 1991–2020 climate normals, the World Meteorological Organization's standard 30-year averages, distributed by NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information:

- **Sydney Observatory Hill**, WMO 94768, 39 m, by the harbour. Not the airport.
- **Canberra Airport**, WMO 94926, 577 m, inland. Australia's capital, and the reference table on our [Australia sheet](/australia/).
- **Wellington Aero AWS**, WMO 93439, 4 m, on Cook Strait. New Zealand's capital, and the reference table on our [New Zealand sheet](/new-zealand/).

Three stations do not describe two countries. Australia's tropical north and its desert are not in this table, and nor are Auckland, Queenstown or anywhere else on New Zealand's South Island. Where the official tourism bodies say something about those places, we quote them below. Where nobody said anything, we leave it blank.

Two things jump out. First, the seasons are the reverse of North America's and Europe's: the warm end of every column is December to February, and Canberra's average low reaches **0.0 °C in July**. Tourism Australia puts it plainly: "our seasons are the opposite to the Northern Hemisphere." Second, the three stations fail for different reasons. Sydney fails mostly on rain, Canberra only on cold, and Wellington almost always on cold.

## Why January wins

January is the month when the three tables overlap. Sydney's average high is 27.4 °C with 91.1 mm, just under the rain line. Canberra's is 29.4 °C, its warmest month, just under the heat line, with 57.4 mm. Wellington reaches 21.0 °C with 60.0 mm, which makes January its driest month.

The margins are thin, and they are worth knowing. Sydney passes January by 8.9 mm. Canberra passes it by 0.6 °C. If you are the kind of traveller who reads "average high 29.4 °C" as "too hot for walking", Canberra in January is at the very top of what our rule allows.

## December and February: one station short each

**February** keeps Wellington and Canberra but loses Sydney. Wellington's average high of 21.2 °C is the highest in its table, and Canberra is at 27.9 °C, but Sydney's precipitation jumps to 131.5 mm, the second-wettest month in its table after June. If your trip is New Zealand-heavy, February is as good as January on our numbers. If it is Sydney-heavy, it is not.

**December** keeps both Australian stations and loses Wellington, by the smallest possible margin in this story: its average high is 19.6 °C, 0.4 °C short of the line. A 20 °C cut-off is a rule, not a fact about how warm Wellington feels, and Tourism New Zealand's own figures treat December as summer (more on that below).

**March** is a Canberra month only. Sydney is too wet (117.5 mm) and Wellington's average high has already dropped to 19.7 °C.

## What the tourism bodies say

Both national tourism organisations publish their own seasonal guides, and both line up with the station tables, with details our three stations cannot give.

**Tourism Australia** lists summer as December through February, with average daily temperatures of about 20 °C to 37 °C in the major capital cities; autumn as March through May; winter as June through August, from about 11 °C in the south to 30 °C in the north; and spring as September through November. It calls December to February the high season and "the most expensive time to travel in Australia", and suggests considering February, after school has gone back. It calls the shoulder seasons, March to May and September to November, "among the best times to travel the entire country", and says June to August is the low season, with fewer tourists, except in Cairns and the Great Barrier Reef region, where it is peak season.

It also warns about a part of Australia that none of our tables covers. In the tropical north, its page says, there are two seasons: the **wet season, between October and April**, and the **dry season, from May to September**, with an average maximum around 35 °C. So the January that wins on our three stations is wet season in the tropics. If your Australia leg means Cairns or the Top End rather than Sydney and Canberra, Tourism Australia's own guidance points the other way: to the middle of the year.

**Tourism New Zealand** gives seasonal minimum and maximum temperatures for four cities. For summer, December to February, it lists Auckland at 17–24 °C, Wellington 14–20 °C, Queenstown 9–23 °C and Christchurch 13–23 °C. For winter, June to August, it lists Queenstown at −1 °C to 8 °C and Christchurch at −2 °C to 11 °C. Those are rounded seasonal ranges for each city, not the station normal on our sheet, so they do not match our Wellington table to the decimal point; we print both and do not merge them. The same page says that "even after summer officially ends in February", autumn brings "some of the best weather of the year", and that attractions are open year-round.

Put together: January and February are the months in which both countries' summer and our weather rule agree. Tourism Australia's note that December to February is also its most expensive season is the trade-off.

## What changes when you cross the Tasman

A two-country trip means two sets of practical facts. Here is what our sheets say, each line linked to its source on the sheet.

**Same in both countries**

- **Plugs.** The IEC world-plugs list shows both [Australia](/australia/#plugs) and [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#plugs) at **Type I, 230 V, 50 Hz**, so the plug that fits one fits the other. Our [plug list](/plug-types-by-country/) groups every place on the site by type.
- **Driving side.** Both drive on the **left**. Tourism Australia's road-safety page says Australians drive on the left side of two-way roads and suggests asking passengers to remind you when you set off and when turning; Tourism New Zealand's driving page says "we drive on the left side of the road in New Zealand." Both are on our [drive-on-the-left list](/countries-that-drive-on-the-left/).
- **Travel advisory.** The U.S. State Department lists both at **Level 1, Exercise Normal Precautions**; see the [Australia](/australia/#advisory) and [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#advisory) sheets for the dates.
- **Tap water.** CDC's traveler pages for [Australia](/australia/#water) and [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#water) do not give a yes-or-no answer on tap water, and we do not turn that into one.

**Different**

- **Currency.** Australia uses the **Australian dollar (AUD)** and New Zealand the **New Zealand dollar (NZD)**, per ISO 4217. They are separate currencies ([Australia](/australia/#money), [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#money)).
- **Emergency number.** The UK Foreign Office lists **000** for ambulance, fire and police in [Australia](/australia/#emergency) and **111** in [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#emergency). Neither is 911. Our [112 story](/journal/112-emergency-number/) covers which countries use which number.
- **Time zones.** Sydney is on the IANA zone **Australia/Sydney**, one of 12 Australian rows in the time-zone database; New Zealand is **Pacific/Auckland** for most of the country, plus Pacific/Chatham for the Chatham Islands ([Australia](/australia/#time), [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#time)). We name the zones and do not build a converter.
- **Tipping.** Tourism New Zealand says tipping is not obligatory, even in restaurants and bars, and that hotels and restaurants do not add service charges ([New Zealand](/new-zealand/#tipping)). We have **no official tipping line for Australia** yet, so the [Australia sheet](/australia/) leaves it blank.
- **Health.** CDC's Australia page recommends hepatitis B vaccine for unvaccinated travellers younger than 60 and Japanese encephalitis vaccine for people staying a month or more in, or often travelling to, areas with the disease. Its New Zealand page also recommends hepatitis B for that group and says to consider hepatitis A for most travellers ([Australia](/australia/#health), [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#health)). This is a summary, not medical advice.
- **Entry.** There are two separate immigration systems: Australia's Department of Home Affairs and Immigration New Zealand. We link to both from the [Australia](/australia/#entry) and [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#entry) sheets and do not restate visa rules.

## Adding a South Pacific stop

If you are thinking of adding an island stop, our newest sheet, [French Polynesia](/french-polynesia/), uses the Faa'a station at Tahiti's airport, and its numbers run almost opposite to Sydney's and Wellington's: Faa'a passes our rule only from **June to September**, its driest stretch, while December and January there are its wettest months, at 279.1 mm and 267.6 mm. A single January trip that is ideal for Sydney and Wellington is the rainiest time of year in Tahiti on our table.

## Questions people ask

**What is the best month to visit Australia and New Zealand together?** On our three stations, January: it is the only month all three pass. February and December each have two of three.

**Is December a good time to visit Australia and New Zealand?** Sydney and Canberra pass in December. Wellington misses our rule by 0.4 °C, and Tourism New Zealand counts December as summer.

**When should I avoid a combined trip?** From May to August, none of our three stations passes: Canberra and Wellington are too cool, and Sydney is too wet in May, too wet and too cool in June, and too cool in July and August. Tourism Australia says that is also its low season, with fewer tourists, except in Cairns and the Great Barrier Reef.

**Is January hot in Australia?** Canberra's average high in January is 29.4 °C and Sydney's 27.4 °C. Tourism Australia gives summer averages of about 20 °C to 37 °C across the major capital cities, so other cities run hotter than our two stations.

**Do I need a different adapter for New Zealand?** Not on the IEC data: both countries use Type I at 230 V and 50 Hz.

## Our take

For a single trip that covers Sydney, Canberra and Wellington, **January** is the month to beat, and **February** is just as good if you will spend more of the trip in New Zealand than in Sydney. December works for the Australian half. Go in the middle of the year only if the trip is built around Australia's tropical north, which, by Tourism Australia's own account, is a different climate entirely. Whatever the month, pack one Type I adapter, remember to keep left, and save **000** and **111** in your phone before you land.
