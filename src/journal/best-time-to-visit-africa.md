**Short answer:** Africa reaches well into both hemispheres, so there is no single best month. If you want one answer for a trip that crosses it, it is **October or November**. Those are the only two months in which five of the seven African weather stations on our sheets pass the plain rule our month lists use: [Rabat](/morocco/#weather), [Marrakech](/morocco/marrakech/#weather), [Cairo](/egypt/#weather), [Cape Town](/south-africa/#weather) and [Mauritius](/mauritius/#weather). The two that fail, [Dar es Salaam](/tanzania/#weather) and the [Seychelles](/seychelles/#weather), fail on heat and rain, and their best month is **July**.

That is an editorial call, made from seven tables. Here is how we made it, and where the tables stop.

## The rule, and the seven stations

Our [month lists](/best-places-to-visit-in-october/) use one rule: a place passes in a month when its **average daily high is from 20 °C to 30 °C** and it gets **less than 100 mm** of precipitation. Nothing else counts. We have no sourced data on prices, crowds, safari sightings or festivals, so they are not in the count.

We have six African country sheets and one African city sheet. Each prints a single named station with its 1991–2020 monthly normals, distributed by NOAA NCEI:

| Sheet | Station | Height | What it is |
|---|---|---:|---|
| [Morocco](/morocco/) | Rabat-Salé | 75 m | The capital, on the Atlantic. |
| [Marrakech](/morocco/marrakech/) | Marrakech | 464 m | Inland, at the foot of the Atlas. |
| [Egypt](/egypt/) | Cairo (A.P.) | 64.12 m | Cairo's airport. Not Luxor, Aswan or the Red Sea. |
| [South Africa](/south-africa/) | Cape Town International Airport | 42 m | A winter-rain Cape table. Not Johannesburg or Kruger. |
| [Tanzania](/tanzania/) | Dar es Salaam | 53 m | The main coastal city. Not the Serengeti or Kilimanjaro. |
| [Mauritius](/mauritius/) | Plaisance | 50 m | The airport, in the south-east. |
| [Seychelles](/seychelles/) | Seychelles International Airport | 3 m | On Mahé, the only Seychelles file. |

Three of the seven are in North Africa, one is at the continent's southern tip, and three, Dar es Salaam, Plaisance and Mahé, are in the tropics by their NCEI positions. By the same files, Rabat sits at 34°03′ N and Cape Town at 33°57′ S, almost the same distance from the equator on opposite sides, so their summers fall six months apart.

Every month has at least one passing station, and no month has all seven. The pattern is a seesaw. From June to September, North Africa's inland cities overheat and Cape Town drops into winter, while the Indian Ocean side has its dry, cooler months. In October and November both ends are in their shoulder seasons at once.

## North Africa: a spring and an autumn, and a summer that is too hot

Rain is almost never the problem in the north. [Cairo's](/egypt/) table prints less than 7 mm in every month, and 0 mm from June to September. [Marrakech](/morocco/marrakech/) never gets more than 35.2 mm, in March. The problem is heat.

- **Cairo** passes in February, March and April, then again in October, November and December. From May to September the average high is 32.19 °C to 35 °C. October scrapes through at 29.98 °C, which is how the file prints it, to two decimals. January misses at 18.93 °C.
- **Marrakech** passes February to May and October to December. Its summer is the hottest of the seven tables: 37.7 °C in July and 37.4 °C in August. Winter nights are cold, with an average low of 5.9 °C in January.
- **Rabat** is the gentlest. The Atlantic keeps its highest average high at 27.8 °C, in August, so it passes for nine straight months, March to November. It fails only from December to February, on temperature, with highs of 17.4 °C to 18.6 °C. July has 0.4 mm of rain.

So for Morocco and Egypt together, the safe windows are **March and April** or **October and November**, when Rabat, Marrakech and Cairo all pass. Our [Europe story](/journal/best-time-to-visit-europe/) covers the Mediterranean's north shore.

## Cape Town: the opposite calendar

[South Africa's](/south-africa/) station is a winter-rain table, and its year runs the other way. It passes for eight months, **October to May**, with highs from 20.6 °C to 27.3 °C. From June to September it fails on temperature, with highs of 17.9 °C to 19.6 °C. Rain never breaks the rule: the wettest month, June, has 92.3 mm, and January has 9.4 mm.

South African Tourism, the country's official tourism body, says the same thing in words. Its best-times page, read on 23 November 2026, says that in Cape Town "the best time to visit is in spring and autumn, which is April and May or the beginning of September to October", and that winter in Cape Town is less attractive because of wet and windy spells when cold fronts pass through. It says summer runs from November to February. Our table is a little stricter about September: its 19.6 °C average high misses our line by less than half a degree.

The same page says the safari provinces work on a different clock. For Mpumalanga and Limpopo it recommends winter, when days are sunny and warm, nights are dry and cool, and late winter brings animals to the waterholes. We have no table for the Lowveld, so we cannot score that advice. It also lists whale watching from May to November and the Namaqualand spring flowers from late July to October.

## East Africa and the islands: July, and not much else

The three tropical stations are warm all year. Their months turn on rain, and on a few tenths of a degree.

- **[Dar es Salaam](/tanzania/)** passes only in **June and July**. June averages exactly 30 °C with 31.3 mm; July 29.7 °C with 15.8 mm. August has even less rain, 17.8 mm, but its 30.1 °C average high is a tenth over the line. The wettest month is April, at 263.6 mm.
- **[Mauritius](/mauritius/)** passes for six months, **June to November**. Plaisance is the coolest tropical station of the three, with highs of 24.6 °C to 30.2 °C, and October is its driest month, at 49.7 mm. February is its wettest, at 263.0 mm.
- **The [Seychelles](/seychelles/)** pass only in **July**, at 28.7 °C and 79.9 mm. Every other month has more than 100 mm, and January has 426.6 mm, the wettest month in any of the seven tables.

Tanzania National Parks (TANAPA), the government park authority, splits the year into the same blocks the Dar es Salaam table shows. Its trip-planning page, read on 23 November 2026, names a dry season from June to October, which it calls easier for wildlife spotting around water sources; short rains in November and December, with greener scenery and fewer crowds; and long rains from March to May, when it says to check road conditions and lodge availability. The Dar es Salaam table matches that split: March to May have 169.6 mm, 263.6 mm and 172.2 mm, and June to October all stay under 80 mm. TANAPA says Tanzania has varied micro-climates; we have no table for the northern parks, so we do not score the Serengeti.

## Our picks

- **One window for a trip that crosses the continent:** October or November (Rabat, Marrakech, Cairo, Cape Town and Mauritius all pass).
- **Morocco and Egypt together:** March and April, or October and November.
- **Cape Town:** October to May; South African Tourism's own pick is April and May or September and October.
- **Coastal Tanzania:** June or July.
- **Mauritius:** June to November, with October the driest.
- **Seychelles:** July is the only month that passes.
- **The months to avoid for North Africa's inland cities:** June to September, on heat.

## Before you go

The plugs are a patchwork. Our sheets list Types C and E at 127 V and 220 V in [Morocco](/morocco/#plugs); C and F at 220 V in [Egypt](/egypt/#plugs); C, M and N at 230 V in [South Africa](/south-africa/#plugs); D and G at 230 V in [Tanzania](/tanzania/#plugs); C and G at 230 V in [Mauritius](/mauritius/#plugs); and Type G at 240 V in the [Seychelles](/seychelles/#plugs). Our [plug list](/plug-types-by-country/) has them side by side.

The emergency numbers are just as mixed. As printed on our sheets from the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office's lists, Morocco uses police 190 and ambulance and fire 150; Egypt police 122, ambulance 123 and fire 180; South Africa police 10111 and ambulance and fire 10177; Tanzania 112; the Seychelles 999; and Mauritius 999, with 112, 114 and 115 also listed. Our [112 story](/journal/112-emergency-number/) explains where that number works.

Only one of the six countries has an official driving line on our sheets: [South Africa](/south-africa/#driving) drives on the left, and it is on our [drive-on-the-left list](/countries-that-drive-on-the-left/). The others are left blank until we find an official source.

The U.S. State Department's advisories, as our sheets print them, put the Seychelles at Level 1 and Morocco, Egypt, South Africa, Tanzania and Mauritius at Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution". Read the full advisory on each sheet before you book.

## What this does not cover

Seven stations are not a continent. We have no table for Kenya, the Sahara, the Serengeti, Kilimanjaro, Kruger, Johannesburg, Luxor, Zanzibar or Madagascar, and no sheet for most of Africa's countries. Kenya was left out of our sheets because no usable named-station normal was found. We do not rank months on prices, crowds, wildlife or events, because we have no sourced data on them. Every figure above is printed, with its station and its source, on the sheet we linked.
