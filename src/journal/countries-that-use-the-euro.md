**Short answer:** the euro is the official currency of **21 of the 27 European Union countries**. Those 21 are Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia and Spain. The European Central Bank names six more places outside the EU that also use it: **Andorra, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City** under a formal arrangement, and **Montenegro and Kosovo** without one.

The trap for travellers is the other half of that list. **Czechia, Hungary, Poland, Romania, Sweden and Denmark** are all in the EU and do **not** use the euro. Neither do the United Kingdom, Switzerland or Iceland. If your trip runs Vienna to Prague to Budapest, you will cross two currency borders in a week.

The sources are the EU's [Countries using the euro](https://european-union.europa.eu/institutions-law-budget/euro/countries-using-euro_en) page (last updated 12 January 2026), the ECB's [Our money](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/intro/html/index.en.html) page and the ISO 4217 currency lines on our sheets. All were read on 15 November 2026.

Two of those dates matter more than the rest to anyone using an old guidebook. **Croatia** switched in 2023, so anything printed before that will tell you to bring kuna. **Bulgaria** is the newest member, listed by the ECB as using the euro since 2026, and the EU page updated on 12 January 2026 counts it among the 21. Each of our eleven euro-country sheets prints the ISO 4217 line "Euro (EUR)".

## The six EU countries that do not use it

The EU page splits the holdouts into two groups.

**Expected to join once they meet the conditions:** Czechia, Hungary, Poland, Romania and Sweden. The EU says all members except Denmark are required to adopt the euro once they meet the "convergence criteria" agreed in the Maastricht Treaty in 1992, but "there is no timetable" for doing so. In practice, that means no date you can plan around.

**Opted out:** Denmark negotiated an opt-out and kept its own currency.

Four of those six are on our sheets, and they are exactly where travellers get caught:

- [Czechia](/czechia/#money): **Czech Koruna (CZK)**. That covers [Prague](/czechia/prague/).
- [Hungary](/hungary/#money): **Forint (HUF)**. That covers [Budapest](/hungary/budapest/).
- [Poland](/poland/#money): **Zloty (PLN)**. That covers [Kraków](/poland/krakow/).
- [Sweden](/sweden/#money): **Swedish Krona (SEK)**.

That is the Central European loop many first-time visitors build: Vienna, Prague, Budapest, Kraków. Only Vienna is on the euro.

## Europe outside the EU: more currencies

Our sheets cover five more European countries outside the EU, and none of them uses the euro:

- [United Kingdom](/united-kingdom/#money): **Pound Sterling (GBP)**. The ECB notes that the UK was an EU member from 1973 to 2020 and "did not use the euro".
- [Switzerland](/switzerland/#money): **Swiss Franc (CHF)**. ISO 4217 also lists two other codes in Switzerland's block, WIR Euro (CHE) and WIR Franc (CHW); our sheet names the franc.
- [Iceland](/iceland/#money): **Iceland Krona (ISK)**.
- [Turkey](/turkey/#money): **Turkish Lira (TRY)**, which covers [Istanbul](/turkey/istanbul/).
- [Norway](/norway/#money): **Norwegian Krone (NOK)**.

## The six outsiders that use the euro anyway

The ECB's wording is careful, and worth keeping:

> The micro-states of Andorra, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City also use the euro, on the basis of a formal arrangement with the European Community. Montenegro and Kosovo likewise use the euro, but without a formal arrangement.

So a Rome trip that adds a day in Vatican City, or a Nice trip with an afternoon in Monaco, stays on one currency. We do not have sheets for those six yet, so we are not adding anything beyond what the ECB says.

The ECB also says euro notes and coins are legal tender in the overseas departments, territories and islands that are part of, or associated with, euro area countries. It does not list them on that page, so neither do we.

## Our take: plan by currency border, not by country count

The headline number, 21 countries, makes the euro sound universal. For a typical traveller it is not. Here is how we would use this list:

1. **Count your currency borders before you go.** A London, Paris, Amsterdam trip crosses one (GBP to EUR). A Vienna, Prague, Budapest trip crosses two. A Zürich and Milan trip crosses one.
2. **Ignore old guidebooks for Croatia and Bulgaria.** Both now use the euro, according to the ECB's table.
3. **Do not assume EU membership means the euro.** It is the single most common mistake this list prevents: Prague, Budapest, Kraków, Bucharest, Stockholm and Copenhagen are all in the EU and all on their own currencies.

We have no sourced data on exchange rates, card acceptance or fees, so we are not going to tell you whether to carry cash. What we can tell you is which currency the official sources list, with the code you will see on your bank statement.

## Same currency, different plugs

One more trap: sharing a currency does not mean sharing a socket. Among our euro-country sheets, [Ireland](/ireland/#plugs) lists Type G (the British-style plug), [Italy](/italy/#plugs) lists Types C, F and L, and [France](/france/#plugs) lists Types C and E. Most of the others list C and F. Outside the euro, [Switzerland](/switzerland/#plugs) lists C and J, and the [UK](/united-kingdom/#plugs) lists G. All of these are from the IEC's world-plugs list, and each sheet gives the voltage too.

## Quick answers

**How many countries use the euro?** Twenty-one EU countries, per the EU and the ECB, plus six non-EU users the ECB names: Andorra, Monaco, San Marino, Vatican City, Montenegro and Kosovo.

**Is Croatia on the euro?** Yes. The ECB lists Croatia as using the euro since 2023, and our [Croatia sheet](/croatia/#money) prints Euro (EUR).

**Does Prague use the euro?** No. Czechia's currency is the Czech Koruna (CZK), per ISO 4217 on our [Czechia sheet](/czechia/#money).

**Does Switzerland use the euro?** No. Its currency is the Swiss Franc (CHF), and it is not an EU member.

**Which EU country chose not to adopt the euro?** Denmark, which the EU says negotiated an opt-out.
