**Short answer:** if the question is weather, the best time to visit the Mediterranean is **May, June or September**. Each of those months passes our warm-and-dry rule at eight of the nine Mediterranean coastal stations on our sheets. **October** is close behind, with seven, and it is the month the eastern end of the sea comes into its own. **August** is the trap: only three of the nine pass, because the south and east are too hot. From **December to February** none of them passes at all.

That is an editorial call, made with one rule and nine weather stations. Here is how.

## The rule, and the nine stations

Our [month lists](/best-places-to-visit-in-june/) use one test everywhere: a place passes in a month when its **average daily high is between 20 °C and 30 °C** and it gets **less than 100 mm** of precipitation. We do not have sourced data on crowds, prices, ferry timetables or sea temperature, so they are not in the test, and we have not guessed at them.

We took every sheet whose station sits on or close to the Mediterranean coast:

Eight of the tables are World Meteorological Organization 1991–2020 normals distributed by NOAA NCEI. **Athens is the exception:** its table is the Hellenic National Meteorological Service's Elliniko summary for 1955–2010, an older and longer period, and we flag it wherever it matters. Two of the files have holes. The Naples file leaves the May and July temperatures (and July rain) blank, and the Venice file leaves the March high blank, so those station-months are not rated.

Some Mediterranean places on our sheets are left out on purpose. [Dubrovnik](/croatia/dubrovnik/) prints rain only. The [Montenegro](/montenegro/) sheet uses Podgorica, the inland capital, not the Bay of Kotor. [Seville](/spain/seville/), [Florence](/italy/florence/) and [Madrid](/spain/madrid/) are inland. Our Turkey sheets use Ankara and Istanbul (on the Sea of Marmara), and our Israel, Egypt and Morocco sheets use Jerusalem, Cairo and Rabat on the Atlantic, none of them a Mediterranean coastal station. And we have no table at all for Malta, Crete or [Santorini](/greece/santorini/).

The shape is a camel with two humps: late spring and early autumn, with a dip in high summer. That is the opposite of northern Europe, where July is the peak. For the continent-wide picture, including why June wins for Europe as a whole, see our [best time to visit Europe](/journal/best-time-to-visit-europe/) story.

## May and June: the whole sea at once

May is the only month in which every rated station passes. The spread is wide but comfortable: [Nice](/france/nice/) averages 21.0 °C with 40.3 mm, [Barcelona](/spain/barcelona/) 22.2 °C, [Rome](/italy/rome/) 22.98 °C, [Athens](/greece/athens/) 24.4 °C with 14.3 mm, and [Larnaka](/cyprus/) 27.0 °C with 11.5 mm. Naples is not counted, because its May temperature is blank in the file.

June keeps all of the west and Italy. [Mallorca](/spain/mallorca/) and [Valencia](/spain/valencia/) both average 27.3 °C, Rome 27.09 °C with 18.66 mm, [Naples](/italy/naples/) 27.92 °C, and Athens 29.2 °C with only 5.4 mm. The one station to drop out is Larnaka, which reaches 30.6 °C in June. If your trip runs east of Greece, June is already high summer.

## July and August: the south and east overheat

High summer on the Mediterranean is dry almost everywhere, so the rule fails on heat, not rain. In July six of the eight rated stations still pass, all of them in Spain, France and Italy: Valencia (29.7 °C, 6.7 mm), Mallorca (29.9 °C, 5.1 mm), Barcelona (29.0 °C), Rome (29.56 °C), Nice (27.5 °C) and Venice (29.02 °C). Several of those are only a few tenths of a degree under the line.

In August most of them tip over:

- [Larnaka](/cyprus/): 33.3 °C, the highest August average high of the nine.
- [Athens](/greece/athens/) (Elliniko): 32.2 °C, as in July.
- [Naples](/italy/naples/): 31.26 °C.
- [Mallorca](/spain/mallorca/): 30.4 °C. [Valencia](/spain/valencia/): 30.3 °C. [Rome](/italy/rome/): 30.02 °C, missing by two hundredths.

Only [Barcelona](/spain/barcelona/) (29.7 °C, 51.2 mm), [Nice](/france/nice/) (27.9 °C, 17.2 mm) and [Venice](/italy/venice/) (28.64 °C, 65.76 mm) pass. These are averages of the daily high, so many afternoons run hotter. Larnaka also shows how dry the eastern summer is: from June to September its file prints 8.4 mm of rain in total, and the Deputy Ministry of Tourism of Cyprus describes long, dry summers running from mid-May to mid-October.

## September: the second spring, west to Greece

September brings back almost everyone. Rome drops to 26.43 °C, Athens to 28.3 °C with 12.3 mm, Mallorca to 27.7 °C and Valencia to 27.8 °C. Naples (27.13 °C) and Venice (24.17 °C) pass too. Barcelona averages 26.7 °C, but its September rain, 86.1 mm, is its wettest month of the year: still under the line, but a sign that the autumn storms are on their way. Larnaka is still out, at 31.4 °C.

## October: the east's best month, the Riviera's first wet one

October is where the two ends of the sea part ways. In the west, the rain arrives. [Nice](/france/nice/) jumps from 81.0 mm in September to **127.9 mm in October** and 138.4 mm in November, and drops off the list. Venice falls below 20 °C (18.71 °C).

In the east, October is the sweet spot. [Larnaka](/cyprus/) averages 28.3 °C with 16.0 mm, its first passing month since May, and [Athens](/greece/athens/) 23.4 °C with 45.9 mm. Valencia (24.5 °C), Mallorca (24.4 °C), Rome (22.43 °C), Naples (23.04 °C) and Barcelona (22.7 °C) all still pass. Seven of nine makes October a better Mediterranean month than July or August by this rule.

## April and November: the shoulder for warm-weather hunters

Only three stations pass at each end of the season, and they are the same three: Valencia, Mallorca and Larnaka.

- **April:** Larnaka averages 23.0 °C with 15.8 mm, Valencia 21.1 °C and Mallorca 20.3 °C. Athens (19.6 °C) and Naples (19.56 °C) are just short.
- **November:** Larnaka is the clear leader at 23.3 °C with 42.0 mm; Valencia and Mallorca both average 20.1 °C. Naples crosses 100 mm (115.52 mm), and Nice is at 138.4 mm.

## Winter: mild, not warm

None of the nine passes from December to February. The warmest winter days on our sheets are at the eastern end: Larnaka averages 18.9 °C in December and 17.1 °C in January, against 17.5 °C and 16.5 °C at Palma. But Larnaka is also wetter in winter (95.4 mm in December, 83.2 mm in January). At the other end, Venice averages 7.66 °C in January. For a ranked list of December stations, including sunshine hours, see [the warmest places in Europe in December](/journal/warmest-places-in-europe-in-december/).

## Our picks

- **Best overall:** June for the western Mediterranean and Italy; May for a trip that includes Greece or Cyprus.
- **Best single month for the whole sea:** May (every rated station passes), then June and September (eight of nine).
- **Best for Greece and Cyprus:** May and October. Athens also passes in June and September; Larnaka does not.
- **Best for the Riviera:** May to September. Nice passes in all five months, then fails in October and November on rain.
- **Month to avoid for the south and east:** August, on heat alone.
- **Warmest in winter:** Larnaka, then Palma and Valencia.

## Before you go

All nine places use the euro; see [which countries use the euro](/journal/countries-that-use-the-euro/). Plugs and driving are where they differ. Spain and Greece list Types C and F at 230 V, France C and E, and Italy C, F and L. [Cyprus](/cyprus/#plugs) uses Type G, the British three-pin plug, at 240 V, and it [drives on the left](/countries-that-drive-on-the-left/); the Deputy Ministry of Tourism warns that "Traffic moves on the LEFT hand-side of the road". Our [plug list](/plug-types-by-country/) has every country side by side. 112 works for emergencies in all five countries; our [112 story](/journal/112-emergency-number/) explains where it does and does not. For what official sources say about leaving a tip, see [tipping in Europe](/journal/tipping-in-europe/).

The U.S. State Department rates Cyprus Level 1, and its advisory warns travelers not to enter the United Nations buffer zone anywhere other than a designated crossing point; the [Cyprus sheet](/cyprus/#advisory) has the full line and its date.

## What this does not cover

We have no sea-temperature figures, so this story cannot tell you when the water is warm enough to swim. We have no sourced data on crowds, prices, school holidays, ferry schedules or the summer wind, so the months are not ranked on any of them. A coastal station is one point on one coast; the islands, the hills behind them and the other shore can differ. We have no Mediterranean coastal table for Turkey, Israel, Egypt, Tunisia, Malta, Crete or Croatia's Dalmatian coast, and we have not filled them in. These are 1991–2020 averages (1955–2010 for Athens), not a forecast.
