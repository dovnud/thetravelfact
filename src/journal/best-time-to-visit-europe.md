**Short answer:** if the question is weather, **June** is the best month to visit Europe, with **May and September** close behind. In June, 27 of the 38 European weather stations on our sheets that can be rated pass our warm-and-dry rule. **July and August** still work in the north, but they price the south out on heat: Seville, Madrid, Florence and Athens all average highs above 32 °C. From **December to February**, not one of our European stations passes.

That is an editorial call, and here is exactly how we made it.

## The rule, and why one rule beats a vibe

Most "best time to visit Europe" advice mixes weather with crowds, prices and festivals. We have no sourced data on crowds or prices, so we left them out rather than guess. What we do have is a named weather station on every sheet, with its 1991–2020 monthly averages and a dated source.

So we applied the same rule our [month lists](/best-places-to-visit-in-june/) use: a place passes in a given month when its **average daily high is between 20 °C and 30 °C** and it gets **less than 100 mm** of precipitation. Nothing else counts. Our European sheets cover 40 distinct stations. Two of them cannot be rated: [Santorini](/greece/santorini/) has no table at all, and [Dubrovnik](/croatia/dubrovnik/) prints rain only. That leaves 38. A few have a missing month in their source file, and the count says so.

Most figures are WMO 1991–2020 normals distributed by NOAA NCEI. The UK sheets use Met Office averages. [Athens](/greece/athens/) uses the Hellenic National Meteorological Service's Elliniko table, which covers 1955–2010. Each sheet names its own source.

The curve is lopsided. Europe's good-weather season, by this rule, is a five-month block from May to September, peaking in June.

September, October and November do not change: none of the six passes in those months.

## Why June wins

In June the south has warmed up but has not yet overheated, and the north has finally reached 20 °C. [Rome](/italy/rome/) averages 27.09 °C at Fiumicino with 18.66 mm of rain. [Madrid](/spain/madrid/) averages 29.4 °C with 19.6 mm. [Paris](/france/paris/) averages 23.4 °C, [Amsterdam](/netherlands/amsterdam/) 20.3 °C, [Vienna](/austria/vienna/) 25.1 °C and [Prague](/czechia/prague/) 23.5 °C, all under 100 mm. [London](/united-kingdom/london/) at Heathrow reaches 21.57 °C with 47.25 mm.

June also works at both ends of the continent at once. [Lisbon](/portugal/lisbon/) averages 26.1 °C and 14.1 mm in June, and [Kraków](/poland/krakow/) averages 23.3 °C and 77.0 mm.

## The trap: southern Europe in July and August

July and August still score well overall because the north is at its best. But look at the southern stations:

- [Seville](/spain/seville/): 35.7 °C in July, 35.6 °C in August.
- [Madrid](/spain/madrid/): 33.3 °C in July, 32.6 °C in August.
- [Florence](/italy/florence/): 32.27 °C in July, 32.42 °C in August.
- [Athens](/greece/athens/) (Elliniko): 32.2 °C in both months.
- [Naples](/italy/naples/): 31.26 °C in August.

Those are averages of the daily high, so many afternoons run hotter. If your trip is mainly Spain, Italy or Greece, July and August are the months we would skip. Move it to June or September.

## September: the south's second spring

September is the month we would pick for the Mediterranean. The heat breaks but the rain has not yet arrived. Rome drops to 26.43 °C with 59.79 mm. Madrid drops to 26.9 °C with 23.6 mm. Athens drops to 28.3 °C with 12.3 mm. [Barcelona](/spain/barcelona/) averages 26.7 °C, though its September rain (86.1 mm) is its wettest month of the year.

October is where the coastline turns. [Nice](/france/nice/) jumps from 81.0 mm in September to 127.9 mm in October and 138.4 mm in November. Lisbon goes from 38.6 mm in September to 110.6 mm in October. Florence passes 100 mm in October too (105.10 mm). Only eight stations still pass in October, and all of them are in Spain, Italy or Greece.

## Scandinavia and the Low Countries: a short summer window

The northern capitals do warm up enough, but only briefly. By the rule:

- [Oslo](/norway/) passes in June (20.4 °C, 79.7 mm) and July (22.7 °C, 86.7 mm). August is warm enough at 21.3 °C but crosses 100 mm (102.8 mm).
- [Stockholm](/sweden/) passes in June, July and August, peaking at 23.6 °C in July with 61.5 mm.
- [Helsinki](/finland/) passes only in July (21.9 °C, 57.1 mm) and August (20.5 °C, 81.4 mm). Its June high is 18.8 °C.
- [Belgium](/belgium/) at Uccle passes June to August, from 21.2 °C in June to 23.2 °C in July.

**July is the only month all three Nordic capitals pass.** If a trip strings Oslo, Stockholm and Helsinki together, that is the month the tables point to.

## The Balkans: Podgorica is too hot in summer, Ljubljana too wet

Two of the newest sheets show how far apart two neighbours can be. [Podgorica](/montenegro/), inland at 49 m, passes only in May (25.8 °C, 98.0 mm). From June to August its average high runs from 30.7 °C to 34.5 °C, and from September the rain returns: 147.1 mm in September and 260.5 mm in November. That table is the capital, not the Bay of Kotor; the sheet says so.

[Ljubljana](/slovenia/) never passes at all. It is warm enough from May to September (21.8 °C to 27.9 °C), but every one of those months has more than 110 mm, with September the wettest at 159.5 mm. The station is in the capital at 299 m, not at Lake Bled or on the coast.

## The north never quite gets there, and that is fine

Some places never pass the rule at all, and it is worth knowing which ones before you plan around warm weather:

- [Edinburgh](/united-kingdom/edinburgh/) peaks at 19.40 °C in July. [Dublin](/ireland/dublin/) peaks at 19.5 °C.
- [Iceland](/iceland/) (Reykjavík) peaks at 14.9 °C in July.
- [Zürich](/switzerland/zurich/) is warm enough from June to August but wet: 127.6 mm in June, 126.0 mm in July and 119.1 mm in August.
- [Munich](/germany/munich/) has the same problem, from 107 mm in May to 116.5 mm in August.
- [Ljubljana](/slovenia/) is the same story again: warm enough from May to September, never under 110 mm.

That is not a reason to skip them. It is a reason to pack a layer for Scotland and Ireland and a rain shell for the Alps. If you are going north, July is its warmest month. Edinburgh, Dublin and Reykjavík all peak in July, and so do Oslo (22.7 °C), Stockholm (23.6 °C) and Helsinki (21.9 °C).

## Winter: chase the Spanish coast, not the sun

No European station on our sheets passes the rule from December to February, so be honest about what a winter trip is. If you want the mildest January, the tables point to Spain and Portugal. [Valencia](/spain/valencia/) averages 16.7 °C, [Mallorca](/spain/mallorca/) 16.5 °C, Seville 16.2 °C and Lisbon 15.1 °C. Lisbon is also wet in winter (103.8 mm in January). Valencia is drier, at 38.6 mm.

At the other end, [Warsaw](/poland/) averages 1.0 °C in January, Kraków 1.6 °C and Prague 2.7 °C. [Helsinki](/finland/) averages -0.7 °C in January and -1.3 °C in February, and [Oslo](/norway/) 0.1 °C in January. Those are highs, not lows.

The one early-spring exception is Seville, the only European station that passes in March (21.5 °C, 46.9 mm). It passes again in November (20.1 °C, 72.2 mm). If you want southern Spain without the heat, those are the months.

## Our picks, month by month

- **Best overall:** June.
- **Best for the Mediterranean:** September, then late May.
- **Best for the UK, Ireland and Scandinavia:** July.
- **Best winter escape:** the Valencia and Mallorca coast.
- **Month to avoid for southern cities:** July and August, on heat alone.

## Before you go

Most of the countries in this story use the euro, but not all of them. Czechia, Hungary, Poland, Switzerland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom keep their own currencies; Finland, Belgium, Slovenia and Montenegro use the euro. Our story [on which countries use the euro](/journal/countries-that-use-the-euro/) lists them. Plugs differ too. The [UK](/united-kingdom/#plugs) and [Ireland](/ireland/#plugs) use Type G, [Switzerland](/switzerland/#plugs) lists Type C and J, and [Italy](/italy/#plugs) lists C, F and L.

## What this does not cover

We do not have sourced data on crowds, prices, school holidays or events, so this story does not rank months on them. A weather station is one point: the coast, the mountains and the other end of a country can differ. Every figure here is printed, with its station and its source, on the sheet we linked.
