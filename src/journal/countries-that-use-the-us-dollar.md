**Short answer:** outside the 50 states, the US dollar is a currency of **eight countries and ten territories**, according to the ISO 4217 currency list. The countries that list *only* the dollar are **Ecuador, Timor-Leste, the Marshall Islands, Micronesia and Palau**. **El Salvador, Panama and Haiti** list the dollar *alongside* a currency of their own. The territories are **Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa, the Northern Mariana Islands, the US Minor Outlying Islands, the British Virgin Islands, the Turks and Caicos Islands, Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba**, and the **British Indian Ocean Territory**.

The trap for American travellers is the list of places that look as if they should be on it and are not. **The Bahamas, Jamaica, Aruba, the Dominican Republic, Costa Rica and Mexico** each have their own currency in ISO 4217. A beach two hours from Miami is not automatically a dollar beach.

Our source for the list is ISO 4217 List One, the international currency-code standard, as published by its maintenance agency, SIX, on 17 September 2026. We read it on 17 November 2026. It is the same file every one of our [destination sheets](/) uses for its currency line.

## The countries: dollar only, or dollar alongside

ISO 4217 gives one row per currency per country. Where a country has two rows, both are legal currencies in the standard's eyes, and we print both.

| Country | What ISO 4217 lists | Notes from official sources |
|---|---|---|
| Ecuador | US Dollar (USD) | Only the dollar is listed. |
| El Salvador | El Salvador Colon (SVC) and US Dollar (USD) | Our [El Salvador sheet](/el-salvador/#money) prints both. See the 2001 law below. |
| Panama | Balboa (PAB) and US Dollar (USD) | Our [Panama sheet](/panama/#money) prints both. |
| Haiti | Gourde (HTG) and US Dollar (USD) | No sheet yet. |
| Timor-Leste | US Dollar (USD) | Local centavo coins, see below. |
| Marshall Islands | US Dollar (USD) | Only the dollar is listed. |
| Micronesia (Federated States of) | US Dollar (USD) | Only the dollar is listed. |
| Palau | US Dollar (USD) | Only the dollar is listed. |

Two of these are worth a closer look, because their own central banks explain how the arrangement works.

**El Salvador.** The Banco Central de Reserva's history page says the *Ley de Integración Monetaria* was approved on 30 November 2000 and took effect on **1 January 2001**. It fixed the rate at **8.75 colones to the dollar** and gave the dollar unrestricted legal tender. The same page says the law, in its article 5, kept colón notes and coins issued before it as permanent legal tender, but required banks to exchange them for dollars when presented. That is why ISO still has a colón row.

**Timor-Leste.** The Banco Central de Timor-Leste says plainly that "the official currency of Timor-Leste is the United States dollar". Since **10 November 2003** it has issued its own *centavo* coins, worth the same as US cents: 100 Timor centavos equal 100 US cents. The bank's page does not say those coins are accepted outside the country, so do not count on spending them at home.

**Panama** is the one most travellers will actually visit, and our sheet prints the ISO line exactly: "Balboa (PAB) and US Dollar (USD)." The [Panama sheet](/panama/) also has the plug line (Types A and B, 110 V, 60 Hz, from the IEC's world-plugs list), so a US charger needs no adapter there. Driving is on the right, per Executive Decree 640 of 2006, and the single time zone is America/Panama. Panama does not have a climate table on our site yet, so it is not rated on the month lists.

We have no official source on the day-to-day mix of balboa and dollar cash in Panama, or of gourde and dollar in Haiti, so we are not going to guess at it.

## The territories that use the dollar

ISO 4217 gives these territories their own rows, each with US Dollar (USD):

- **Puerto Rico.** Our [Puerto Rico sheet](/puerto-rico/#money) prints US Dollar (USD) from its own ISO entry. Plugs are Type A and B at 120 V, 60 Hz, and Discover Puerto Rico, the island's official destination site, says driving is on the same side as the mainland. Entry links to CBP's page for international visitors, because Puerto Rico is a US territory.
- **US Virgin Islands.** Our [US Virgin Islands sheet](/us-virgin-islands/#money) prints US Dollar (USD). Plugs are Type A and B at 110 V, 60 Hz, from the IEC. Entry links to CBP's page for international visitors, as with Puerto Rico. The travel advisory is “Not applicable,” because it is a US territory.
- **Guam**, **American Samoa**, the **Northern Mariana Islands** and the **US Minor Outlying Islands**.
- **British Virgin Islands** and **Turks and Caicos Islands**, two British overseas territories that ISO lists on the dollar.
- **Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba**, the Caribbean Netherlands. That is a separate ISO entry from **Aruba** (Aruban Florin, AWG) and from **Curaçao** and **Sint Maarten**, which ISO lists on the Caribbean Guilder (XCG).
- **British Indian Ocean Territory**.

If you are counting "places," that is 18 non-US entries plus the United States itself, 19 ISO rows with the dollar in total.

Inside the United States, every one of our sheets prints the same line: [New York](/united-states/new-york/#money), [Miami](/united-states/miami/#money), [Los Angeles](/united-states/los-angeles/#money), [San Francisco](/united-states/san-francisco/#money), [Hawaii](/hawaii/#money), [Honolulu](/hawaii/honolulu/) and [Maui](/hawaii/maui/). ISO 4217 also lists a "US Dollar (Next day)" code, USN, under the United States. That is a funds code used in finance, not a currency you will see in a shop, so our sheets leave it out.

## The near misses: dollars in the name, not on the list

These are the places where we see the most confusion, and every one of them has its own sheet with its own ISO line.

- [The Bahamas](/bahamas/#money): **Bahamian Dollar (BSD)**. ISO gives the Bahamas one currency, and it is not the US dollar. The Bahamas is also the rare near-neighbour that [drives on the left](/countries-that-drive-on-the-left/): the Ministry of Tourism says UK rules apply.
- [Jamaica](/jamaica/#money): **Jamaican Dollar (JMD)**.
- [Aruba](/aruba/#money): **Aruban Florin (AWG)**.
- [Dominican Republic](/dominican-republic/#money): **Dominican Peso (DOP)**, which also covers [Punta Cana](/dominican-republic/punta-cana/).
- [Costa Rica](/costa-rica/#money): **Costa Rican Colon (CRC)**.
- [Mexico](/mexico/#money): **Mexican Peso (MXN)**, for [Cancún](/mexico/cancun/), [Mexico City](/mexico/mexico-city/) and the rest.
- [Canada](/canada/#money): **Canadian Dollar (CAD)**, the other "dollar" most Americans meet first.

Further afield, ISO lists [Colombia](/colombia/#money) on the Colombian Peso (COP), [Peru](/peru/#money) on the Sol (PEN), [Australia](/australia/#money) on the Australian Dollar (AUD), [New Zealand](/new-zealand/#money) on the New Zealand Dollar (NZD) and [Singapore](/singapore/#money) on the Singapore Dollar (SGD).

Two countries often named in "uses the dollar" lists are worth flagging. ISO 4217 lists **Zimbabwe** only on Zimbabwe Gold (ZWG), and **Cambodia** only on the Riel (KHR). Neither has a US dollar row in the 17 September 2026 file. We have not read an official source on how much dollar cash circulates in either, so that is all we will say.

## Can you pay in US dollars in Mexico or Costa Rica?

People search this a lot. The honest answer from the sources we use is narrow: the official currency in Mexico is the peso and in Costa Rica the colón, per ISO 4217. We have no official source that sets out whether shops, taxis or hotels accept dollars, at what rate, or how change is given. Whether a business takes your dollars is that business's choice, and the rate it offers is not something we can check. We would rather tell you the currency on your bank statement than guess at a cashier's habits.

## Our take: what the list changes for a trip

1. **Count currency borders, not miles.** Miami to Nassau crosses one (USD to BSD). San Juan to Saint Thomas crosses none: both [Puerto Rico](/puerto-rico/#money) and the [US Virgin Islands](/us-virgin-islands/#money) are on the dollar. San Juan to Punta Cana crosses one (USD to DOP).
2. **A dollar country is not a US jurisdiction.** Ecuador, Panama and Timor-Leste are on the dollar, but they are separate countries. The State Department does not issue travel advisories for US jurisdictions, which is why our [Puerto Rico](/puerto-rico/#advisory), [Hawaii](/hawaii/#advisory) and [US Virgin Islands](/us-virgin-islands/#advisory) sheets print "Not applicable" on that line; a foreign dollar country gets one. Our [El Salvador sheet](/el-salvador/#advisory) prints the State Department's Level 1, "Exercise Normal Precautions" (feed dated 25 June 2026, read 19 November 2026). Our [Panama sheet](/panama/#advisory) prints the State Department's Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution" (feed dated 19 August 2026, read 15 November 2026).
3. **Check the plug as well as the money.** Panama, Puerto Rico and the Bahamas all list Type A and B plugs at 110–120 V, 60 Hz, from the IEC. That is not true of every dollar place, and our [plug types by country](/plug-types-by-country/) list has every sheet in one table.
4. **Use the month lists to choose when.** By our rule (average high 20–30 °C and under 100 mm in the sheet's own table), [Puerto Rico](/puerto-rico/#weather) passes in [February](/best-places-to-visit-in-february/) and [March](/best-places-to-visit-in-march/). [Hawaii](/hawaii/#weather) passes in seven months, January to May plus [November](/best-places-to-visit-in-november/) and [December](/best-places-to-visit-in-december/). The [Bahamas](/bahamas/#weather) passes January to April, November and December. The Caribbean side of that choice also has a storm season: see our [Caribbean hurricane season](/journal/caribbean-hurricane-season/) story.

## Quick answers

**How many countries use the US dollar?** Outside the United States, ISO 4217 lists the dollar for eight countries: Ecuador, El Salvador, Panama, Haiti, Timor-Leste, the Marshall Islands, Micronesia and Palau. Five of them (Ecuador, Timor-Leste and the three Pacific nations) list only the dollar. It also lists ten territories.

**Does Puerto Rico use the US dollar?** Yes. ISO 4217 lists US Dollar (USD) under Puerto Rico's own entry, and our [Puerto Rico sheet](/puerto-rico/#money) prints it. The same is true of our [US Virgin Islands sheet](/us-virgin-islands/#money).

**Does the Bahamas use the US dollar?** Its ISO currency is the Bahamian Dollar (BSD). There is no US dollar row for the Bahamas.

**Does Panama use the US dollar?** Yes, alongside the balboa. ISO lists both, PAB and USD.

**Since when has El Salvador used the dollar?** Since 1 January 2001, when the *Ley de Integración Monetaria* took effect, according to the Banco Central de Reserva.

**Does Costa Rica use the US dollar?** Its ISO currency is the Costa Rican Colon (CRC). See our [Costa Rica sheet](/costa-rica/#money), which now also has a [month-by-month best-time section](/costa-rica/#best-time).
