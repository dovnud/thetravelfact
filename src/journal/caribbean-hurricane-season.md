**Short answer:** the best time to visit the Caribbean, on the numbers we can source, is **December to April**. That stretch sits entirely outside the Atlantic hurricane season, which runs from **1 June to 30 November**; the National Hurricane Center says the Atlantic basin includes the Caribbean Sea. The statistical peak is **10 September**, with most activity between **mid-August and mid-October**. In the 13 Caribbean-area rain tables on our sheets, **February and March are the only months when every station is under 100 mm**, and August, September and October are the months when only two are (Aruba and Curaçao). That is our editorial view, and the rest of this story shows the numbers behind it.

Everything below comes from two kinds of source. The season dates and storm averages come from the [National Hurricane Center's climatology page](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/), read on 15 November 2026. The rain and temperature figures are the 1991–2020 monthly normals printed on our own destination sheets, and each sheet names its station. This story first ran on 15 November 2026 with eight stations; on 24 November 2026 we added the five Caribbean sheets published since (the [Cayman Islands](/cayman-islands/), [Barbados](/barbados/), [Curaçao](/curacao/), the [US Virgin Islands](/us-virgin-islands/) and [Antigua and Barbuda](/antigua-and-barbuda/)) and a month-by-month count. We have not added any figure that is not on one of those pages.

## The official window, in the hurricane center's words

The NHC puts it plainly: "The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30." It also says tropical cyclones "sometimes" form before and after those dates. So treat the window as the period that covers most storms, not as a guarantee on either end.

The NHC's 1991–2020 averages for a typical Atlantic season are:

- **14 named storms, 7 hurricanes and 3 major hurricanes** (Category 3, 4 or 5).
- The first named storm typically forms in **mid to late June**.
- The first hurricane tends to form in **early to mid-August**.
- The first major hurricane forms in **late August or early September**.

The NHC's own table of benchmark dates makes the shape of the season clearer than any map. In an average year the first named storm has formed by about 20 June, but the first hurricane does not arrive until about 11 August, and the first major hurricane until about 1 September. By 19 November an average season has produced its fourteenth named storm. June and July are early in the curve; most of a typical season happens after the middle of August.

> The NHC's peak date for the Atlantic season is 10 September. Most activity happens between mid-August and mid-October.

That one sentence is the most useful thing a Caribbean trip planner can know. If your dates sit in late August, September or early October, you are travelling through the busiest part of a typical season.

## Mexico's Pacific coast runs on a different clock

If your "Caribbean" trip is actually a Mexican beach trip, check which coast it is on. The NHC gives the **eastern Pacific** season as **15 May to 30 November**, starting two weeks earlier than the Atlantic season. Its 1991–2020 average is 15 named storms, 8 hurricanes and 4 major hurricanes, with the first major hurricane around mid-July.

On our sheets, [Cancún](/mexico/cancun/) is on the Atlantic side. [Cabo San Lucas](/mexico/cabo-san-lucas/) and [Puerto Vallarta](/mexico/puerto-vallarta/) are on the eastern Pacific side. The [Mexico](/mexico/) country sheet prints both seasons.

## What the rain tables say, island by island

Three things stand out.

**March is the driest month at eight of the thirteen stations.** Santo Domingo, Punta Cana, San Juan, Cancún, Grand Cayman, Barbados, St. Thomas and Antigua all bottom out in March. Kingston (February), Nassau (December), Miami (January) and Aruba (April) have their driest month elsewhere in the December-to-April window. Curaçao is the one exception: its driest month is May, at 15.3 mm, with April a hair behind at 15.8 mm.

**The wettest months land inside the hurricane window everywhere.** Every station's wettest month falls between June and November. Five of the thirteen peak in November (San Juan, Aruba, Curaçao, St. Thomas and Antigua), and three in October (Cancún, Grand Cayman and Barbados). At Santo Domingo, August (195.0 mm) and September (191.7 mm) are almost tied. In Kingston, September (184.7 mm) and October (161.8 mm) are well above every other month. San Juan's wettest month is November, near the end of the season.

**Aruba and Curaçao are the outliers in this table.** Aruba's wettest month in the Meteorological Department Aruba summary is November, at 87.1 mm, the only wettest month in the table under 100 mm; its April figure is 8.6 mm. Curaçao's wettest month is November, at 141.0 mm, but its August (27.7 mm) and September (43.0 mm) are drier than most stations' driest months. The lowest wettest month after Aruba's is Antigua's November, at 134.6 mm. The table is about rain, not storms, and we are not claiming anything about Aruba's storm risk. Note also that Aruba's summary does not name a station; [the sheet](/aruba/) says so.

Two Caribbean-area sheets cannot be compared this way. [Panama](/panama/) has no 1991–2020 table on our sheet, so its cells are blank. [Punta Cana](/dominican-republic/punta-cana/) prints rain only, because its temperature rows in the source file did not match the station; that sheet explains why.

Read it two ways.

**On rain alone, February and March are the clear winners.** All 13 stations are under 100 mm in both. January loses San Juan (103.3 mm) and Cancún (103.8 mm); April loses only San Juan (116.9 mm); December loses only San Juan (123.3 mm). From August to October, only Aruba and Curaçao stay under 100 mm.

**On our stricter month-list rule, January and February lead, with eight stations each.** The list drops places whose average high is above 30 °C, so Kingston and Aruba, which run above 30 °C in every month, never make it. From May to October no station passes, partly on rain and partly on heat. In November only Nassau and Miami pass.

May to July is the in-between stretch. About half the stations are still under 100 mm, and the NHC's average first hurricane does not form until early to mid-August, but the average high is above 30 °C at every station that prints temperature.

## Heat is not the deciding factor

If you are choosing between winter and summer for warmth, the tables say it barely matters. Santo Domingo's average daily high runs from 30.0 °C in January and February to 32.4 °C in September. Kingston runs from 30.9 °C to 33.1 °C. Aruba, from 30.3 °C to 33.2 °C. San Juan, from 28.5 °C to 31.8 °C. Of the five newer sheets, Grand Cayman runs from 28.4 °C to 32.3 °C, Barbados from 29.1 °C to 31.0 °C, Curaçao from 30.0 °C to 33.0 °C, St. Thomas from 29.0 °C to 32.1 °C, and Antigua from 28.2 °C to 31.1 °C. Barbados has the narrowest range in the table, 1.9 °C between its coolest and warmest months.

The Bahamas and Miami are the exceptions. Nassau's January high is 26.1 °C, and Miami's is 24.6 °C. Both sit further north. If you want true beach heat in January, the tables point south of them.

## Our call: December to April, with March as the safe bet

Here is the editorial view, stated plainly so you can disagree with it:

1. **Book December to April if you can.** All of it is outside the official Atlantic season, and twelve of the thirteen stations in our table have their driest month in that window (Curaçao's is May, at 15.3 mm, 0.5 mm below its April). If you can pick one month, February and March are the only months in which every station is under 100 mm.
2. **If you must go in summer, June and July are earlier in the curve.** The NHC's average first hurricane forms in early to mid-August, so a June or early July trip sits ahead of the busiest stretch. That is a statement about averages, not a promise about any given year.
3. **Treat mid-August to mid-October as the high-risk window.** That is the NHC's own description of where most activity happens, and 10 September is its peak date.
4. **November is a mixed month.** It is still inside the season, and it is the wettest month at five of the thirteen stations, including San Juan, St. Thomas and Antigua. Late November sits near the end of the NHC's benchmark table, but it is not outside the season.

If your dates are fixed in September, it is worth knowing what the sheets say before you go: the plugs, the driving side and the emergency number are all on each sheet.

## What we do not know, and will not guess

We have no sourced figure for hotel prices, crowds, or cruise schedules in hurricane season, so this story does not mention them. We also do not forecast. The NHC averages describe a typical season, and any single season can be busier or quieter. For a live storm, the only source that matters is the [National Hurricane Center](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/).
